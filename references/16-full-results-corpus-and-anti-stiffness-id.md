## Part 1 — Complete 14-Module Full-Paper Results Positive Expression Atlas

> **How to Use**: Match each Results paragraph's analytical job to Modules `M1–M14` below, pick a verified **Starter + Core Readout + Bridge**, and plug in ONLY your real biological entities and verified results. Keep inline `P/q` and secondary numbers on Figures/Captions (see [`14-anti-ai-prose-stat-restraint-and-rule-harmony.md`](./14-anti-ai-prose-stat-restraint-and-rule-harmony.md)).

### M1 — Clinical Cohort Assembly, Baseline Balance & Radiological/Pathological Readout
- **Skeleton A (Cohort Scope)**: `We assembled a discovery cohort of [N] patients with [disease] treated with [regimen], alongside an independent validation cohort of [M] patients (Fig. 1a; Supplementary Table 1).`
- **Skeleton B (Baseline Balance — Stat-Light)**: `Baseline clinical characteristics, including age, sex, tumour stage and histological grade, were balanced between responders and non-responders (Supplementary Fig. 1b).`
- **Skeleton C (Imaging / Pathological Readout)**: `Serial [MRI / CT] imaging documented complete radiological regression in responding patients, whereas non-responders retained progressive tumour masses despite comparable baseline burden (Fig. 1b).`
- *CNS Verbatim*: `"Pathological response rates were assessed according to microscopic examination of H&E slides of the resected tumor as part of routine clinical practice (Figures 1A and S1A–S1C)."` (*Cell*, 2025)

### M2 — Single-Cell / Single-Nucleus Atlas, Quality Control & Subtype Annotation
- **Skeleton A (Atlas Scale & QC)**: `Single-nucleus RNA sequencing of [N] tumour biopsies yielded [K] high-quality nuclei after ambient RNA removal and quality filtering (Fig. 1d).`
- **Skeleton B (Clustering & Markers)**: `Unsupervised clustering and canonical marker expression resolved [M] major cellular lineages and [S] fine subpopulations spanning malignant, stromal, endothelial and immune compartments (Fig. 1d; Supplementary Fig. 2a).`
- **Skeleton C (Malignant Discrimination)**: `Copy-number inference separated [K] malignant cells from normal diploid epithelial cells across all profiled lesions (Supplementary Fig. 1d).`
- *CNS Verbatim*: `"A total of 137,133 CD8+ TIL were identified, which were further divided into 12 transcriptionally defined subclusters (Figure 4A)."` (*Cancer Cell*, 2025)

### M3 — Differential Expression, Gene Programmes & Pathway Enrichment (GSEA)
- **Skeleton A (Patient-Level Pseudobulk DEG)**: `Patient-level pseudobulk differential expression comparing non-responders with responders identified [N] upregulated and [M] downregulated genes, with [Gene A] among the top induced transcripts (Fig. 4a).`
- **Skeleton B (NMF / Recurrent Transcriptional Programmes)**: `Consensus non-negative matrix factorization across individual tumours recovered [K] recurrent transcriptional programmes that organized into [M] shared malignant states (Fig. 1e).`
- **Skeleton C (GSEA / Pathway Shift)**: `Gene set enrichment analysis revealed strong enrichment of [Pathway A] and [Pathway B] in [Group 1], accompanied by suppression of [Pathway C] (Fig. 1f).`
- *CNS Verbatim*: `"BACH1 expression was downregulated in non-responder tumors and radioresistant cells compared to responders and parental cells (Fig. 5A–C)."` (*Cancer Cell*, 2025)

### M4 — Spatial Microanatomy, Distance Gradients & Cellular Neighbourhoods
- **Skeleton A (Distance Gradient)**: `Across [N] spatially profiled tumours, malignant [Program A] peaked immediately adjacent to blood vessels and declined progressively with distance, whereas [Target] expression increased in distal tumour regions (Fig. 2b,c).`
- **Skeleton B (Cellular Neighbourhoods)**: `Unsupervised cellular neighbourhood analysis partitioned [N] million single cells into [K] reproducible multicellular territories separating tumour, vascular, stromal, lymphoid and myeloid niches (Fig. 2i,j).`
- **Skeleton C (Spatial Segregation / Exclusion)**: `Within [50 µm] of tumour vessels, [Cell A] were markedly depleted, whereas [Cell B] and [Cell C] accumulated along the vascular interface (Fig. 2h).`
- *CNS Verbatim*: `"Within this cohort, Xenium data showed lower representation of intratumoral antigen-presenting cell subsets and exhausted CD8+ T cells in IS tumors (Figure S14A)."` (*Cell*, 2025)

### M5 — Cell–Cell Communication, Ligand–Receptor & Paracrine Niches
- **Skeleton A (Sender–Receiver Mapping)**: `Comparing ligand expression across lineages confirmed that [Fibroblasts] and [Macrophages], rather than [Endothelial cells], served as the primary source of [Ligand] (Fig. 4d).`
- **Skeleton B (Co-localized Niche Axis)**: `Cell–cell communication analysis predicted selective [Ligand–Receptor] signalling between [Sender] and malignant cells within [Niche X], which we confirmed in intact tissue sections (Fig. 4e,f).`
- **Skeleton C (Paracrine Proximity Effect)**: `Epithelial cells situated within [50 µm] of [Marker]+ stromal cells displayed higher [Program A] and lower [Target] expression than distal cells from the same tumour (Fig. 4h).`
- *CNS Verbatim*: `"sEV treatment did not alter the frequency of b2+ IMs in the lungs (Figures S6F and S6G), suggesting that paracrine tumor-derived factors drive..."` (*Cell*, 2025)

### M6 — Trajectory, Lineage Plasticity & Clonal/Longitudinal Dynamics
- **Skeleton A (Continuum & Plasticity)**: `Malignant cells spanned a transcriptional continuum from [differentiated State A] to [mesenchymal/basal State B], inversely coupled to [Target] expression at the patient level (Fig. 1g).`
- **Skeleton B (Pseudotime & Velocity)**: `Trajectory reconstruction and RNA velocity indicated that [State B] emerged progressively from [Progenitor A] along the differentiation axis (Fig. 3a).`
- **Skeleton C (Longitudinal Pre→Post Persistence)**: `In paired pre- and post-treatment biopsies, the [high-plasticity state] persisted or expanded in residual non-responding lesions (Fig. 1h; Supplementary Fig. 5g).`
- *CNS Verbatim*: `"These trajectories were supported by marker genes expressed along their pseudotime and confirmed using RNA velocity, an independent method (Figure 3B)."` (*Cancer Cell*, 2025)

### M7 — Genomics, Copy Number, Epigenetics (scATAC) & Molecular Subtypes
- **Skeleton A (Subtype Alignment)**: `Alignment against established consensus molecular subtypes mapped [State A] onto [Subtype 1] and [State B] onto [Subtype 2], even after removing shared signature genes (Fig. 1j).`
- **Skeleton B (Chromatin / Epigenetics)**: `Increased chromatin accessibility at [enhancer/locus] closely paralleled transcriptional induction of [Gene/Program] across malignant states (Fig. 3e).`
- **Skeleton C (Genomic Confounder Check)**: `Recurrent driver mutations and chromosomal alterations were distributed similarly between groups, excluding a purely genetic basis for [phenotype] (Supplementary Fig. 2c).`
- *CNS Verbatim*: `"As expected, increased chromatin accessibility of the ERVs was generally associated with increased ERV expression (Figure 5E)."` (*Cell*, 2025)

### M8 — *In Vitro* Perturbation, Dose–Response (`IC50`), Co-Culture & Organoids
- **Skeleton A (Perturbation + Transcriptional Readout)**: `Induction of [EMT / pathway] in both [Line A] and [Line B] cells suppressed [Target] expression while upregulating canonical mesenchymal markers (Fig. 5a,b).`
- **Skeleton B (Drug Sensitivity / IC50)**: `Dose–response viability assays demonstrated that [perturbation] increased the IC50 of [Drug] across independent cell lines (Fig. 5c).`
- **Skeleton C (Functional Dependency / Pharmacogenomics)**: `Across [N] cancer cell lines in the absence of stroma, high [State A] correlated with increased genetic dependency on [Gene] knockout and greater sensitivity to [Inhibitor] (Fig. 4c).`
- *CNS Verbatim*: `"Notably, it also improved killing in Karpas-422, a cell line resistant to single-agent CART19 (Figure 1C)."` (*Cancer Cell*, 2025)

### M9 — *In Vivo* Murine Efficacy, Genetic Rescue, Combination Synergy & Endpoint RNA-seq
- **Skeleton A (Genetic Knockdown/KO + Drug)**: `Whereas [Drug] monotherapy modestly slowed tumour growth and [Gene] knockdown alone had minimal effect, combining [Gene] knockdown with [Drug] induced net tumour regression (Fig. 5f,g).`
- **Skeleton B (Pharmacological Combination & Synergy)**: `Pharmacological blockade of [Target] with [Inhibitor] synergized with [Drug] to regress orthotopic tumours across all treated mice (interaction P = [val]; Fig. 5h,i).`
- **Skeleton C (Endpoint Transcriptomics & Immune Reprogramming)**: `Bulk RNA sequencing of endpoint tumours showed that combination therapy suppressed adaptive [Pathway A] while activating interferon signalling and cytotoxic T-cell transcripts (Fig. 5j).`
- *CNS Verbatim*: `"Tumor growth was significantly reduced in avapritinib-treated mice bearing PPK tumors (Figures 3B and 3D)."` (*Cancer Cell*, 2025)

### M10 — Clinical Biomarker Stratification, Survival & Multivariable Cox/ROC
- **Skeleton A (Survival Separation)**: `In cross-validation across [N] patients with survival follow-up, high [Signature/Model] score was strongly associated with prolonged disease-free survival (C-index = [val], P = [val]; Fig. 3h).`
- **Skeleton B (Multivariable Independence)**: `In multivariable Cox regression adjusting for clinical [Marker], stage, age and sex, [Model] remained an independent predictor of outcome (HR = [val], 95% CI [low–high], P = [val]; Fig. 3i).`
- **Skeleton C (Head-to-Head ROC Benchmark)**: `Combining [Feature A] and [Feature B] outperformed single-marker quantification in both discovery and independent validation cohorts (validation AUC = [val]; Fig. 3g).`
- *CNS Verbatim*: `"Univariate Cox regression analysis revealed that s3-CAFs significantly correlated with poor overall survival across combined cohorts (Figure 6A)."` (*Cancer Cell*, 2025)

### M11 — Multi-Cohort Clinical-Trial Validation & Pan-Cancer Generality *(Zero `GSE` Catalog Fatigue!)*
- **Skeleton A (Multi-Cohort Replication — Name Clinical Context, Not 10 `GSE` IDs)**: `We next asked whether this axis generalises across independent patient cohorts. Across multiple independent [cancer type] cohorts totalling [N] patients, the inverse association between [Plasticity] and [Target] replicated consistently after adjusting for tumour purity (Fig. 6a).`
- **Skeleton B (Treatment-Specific Inversion vs Prognosis)**: `Whereas high [Signature] showed no association with survival in untreated surgical patients (Fig. 6c), it predicted longer survival under neoadjuvant chemotherapy (interaction P = [val]; Fig. 6d,e) and inferior outcomes under checkpoint blockade (Fig. 6b).`
- **Skeleton C (Pan-Cancer Specificity)**: `Across major epithelial cancer types in TCGA, inverse [Target] coupling was selective for stratified epithelial lineages, whereas positive [Axis B] co-regulation was conserved broadly across tumour types (Supplementary Fig. 6a).`
- *CNS Verbatim*: `"This association was validated in multiple independent cohorts across a range of cancers (Figure S3B–D)."` (*Cancer Cell*, 2025)

### M12 — Computational Pathology, Pretrained Models & Multimodal Integration
- **Skeleton A (Cross-Modal Prediction)**: `Pretrained pathology foundation models applied to routine H&E slides accurately predicted macroscopic stromal, vascular and lymphoid compartments across discovery and validation cohorts (Fig. 7b,c).`
- **Skeleton B (Honest Optical / Modality Limit)**: `By contrast, routine H&E morphology alone was not sufficient to distinguish intratumoural [Marker]+ from [Marker]− tumour cells, with accuracy falling near chance across pretrained encoders (Fig. 7c).`
- **Skeleton C (External Transfer Without Retraining)**: `Applied without retraining to [N] external diagnostic whole-slide images, predicted stromal and vascular scores correlated with matched bulk RNA expression and stratified progression-free survival (Fig. 7g,h).`
- *CNS Verbatim*: `"Classifiers developed using gene expression feature inputs significantly outperformed models derived using the next best single data types in 10 out of 12 cohorts (Figure 3A)."` (*Cancer Cell*, 2025)

### M13 — Orthogonal Protein/mIF Validation, Confounder Exclusion (`P4`) & Negative Controls
- **Skeleton A (Orthogonal Protein Check)**: `Multiplex immunofluorescence staining on archival sections confirmed these transcriptional differences at the protein level, contrasting [Marker A]+ nests in responders against [Marker B]+ nests in non-responders (Supplementary Fig. 2b).`
- **Skeleton B (Confounder Exclusion)**: `Within-patient multivariable regression adjusting for spatial distance and transcript depth confirmed that [Sender]+ stroma, but not [Control]+ stroma, remained positively associated with malignant [State] (Fig. 4i).`
- **Skeleton C (Null Control — Crisp `11–17w`)**: `The remaining malignant transcriptional programmes did not differ systematically between responders and non-responders (Supplementary Fig. 2d).`
- *CNS Verbatim*: `"Further, incorporating tazemetostat during CART19 manufacturing did not affect CART19 viability, expansion, or killing capacity (Figures S1E–S1G)."` (*Cancer Cell*, 2025)

### M14 — Biological Logic Bridges Between Modules *(Never Mechanical `We next` Chains OR Cold Method Drops!)*
- **Anomaly Bridge**: `Global transcriptional profiles alone, however, could not explain why tumours with comparable clinical [Marker] staining diverged in response.`
- **Mechanistic Cause Bridge**: `Because circulating therapeutics must extravasate across tumour vessels before engaging malignant cells, we examined how [Target] distributes along the tissue interface.`
- **Scale-to-Function Bridge**: `To identify actionable upstream receptors sustaining this [State A], [Target]-low population without candidate bias, we performed differential expression analysis on patient-level malignant pseudobulk counts.`
- **Causal-Perturbation Bridge**: `To directly test whether [Program A] causes drug resistance in human cancer cells, we induced [pathway/perturbation] across independent cell lines.`
- **External-Generalisation Bridge**: `We next asked whether this [Pathway] axis generalises across independent clinical cohorts and distinguishes drug-specific resistance from generic prognosis.`
- **Clinical-Deployment Bridge**: `Although multiplex imaging resolved spatial predictors of response, routine pathology relies on standard H&E slides; we therefore evaluated which tissue compartments can be inferred directly from routine histology.`

## Part 2 — Defeating Stiffness ("Anti-Robotic Flow") in Introduction & Discussion

Unlike Results, **Introduction and Discussion cannot be filled mechanically from fixed templates** — trying to force fixed outlines onto them is precisely what produces stiff, predictable AI prose! Eliminate these **6 exact stiffness traps**:

### 2.1 Three Introduction Stiffness Traps & Natural Human Fixes

| Stiffness Trap (❌ Typical LLM Draft) | Why Editors Immediately Spot It | **Organic Human CNS Pattern (✅)** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. "Bolted-On" Final Sentence in P3** (`"Addressing these questions also requires evaluating pathology models..."`) | Feels like a checklist forcing a late Figure into a biological paragraph that was never about AI! | **One Unified Mystery + Dedicated Short Bridge When Needed**: Keep P3 focused on ONE coherent biological/mechanistic gap; if the paper has a major translational/computational pillar, give it its own crisp 2-sentence bridge paragraph (P4 before the `Here, we...` preview) rather than tacking `"also requires"` onto P3! |
| **2. Final Intro Para "Mini-Abstract Clone" Over-Spoiling** (listing exact `30–50 µm`, internal codes, model counts, `%` readouts inside Intro P4/P5) | Spoils the discovery arc before Results even starts and duplicates Abstract! | **Tease Conceptual Advance Only**: State the integrated approach cleanly and preview the *biological principle* (e.g., `"Here, we integrate single-nucleus, spatial and functional profiling to show that localized niche signalling decouples delivery from target availability..."`) — keep exact distances, internal codes, and model counts for Results! |
| **3. Alphabet-Soup Front Matter** (`> 8` acronyms/100w + Results-only codes in Title/Abstract/Intro) | Exhausts generalist editors in the first 30 seconds (`Nat Commun` med = `1.2` in Abstract, `4.6` in Intro!) | Keep **Title `0–1` acronym**, **Abstract `≤ 5–7/100w` (ZERO internal codes)**, **Intro `≤ 6–7/100w` (define ONLY acronyms reused `≥ 3×`)** — read the first page aloud: if it sounds like code instead of biology, spell out concepts! |

### 2.2 Three Discussion Stiffness Traps & Natural Human Fixes

| Stiffness Trap (❌ Typical LLM Draft) | Empirical Reality (`n = 1,401` CNS Papers) | **Organic Human CNS Pattern (✅)** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Multi-Paragraph `First, ... Second, ... Third, ...` PPT Enumeration** (e.g., P2, P4, AND P6 all structured `First... Second... Third...`) | **`0.0%` of *Nat Commun*** (`0/175`) and **`0.1%` of ALL CNS** (`2/1,401`) repeat `First/Second` across 3 paragraphs! | **Thematic Essay Paragraphs (Max ONE `First/Second` para in the ENTIRE Discussion!)**: Devote each Discussion paragraph to **ONE deep biological or translational question** with fluid internal transitions (`Whereas earlier studies...`, `Beyond this mechanism...`, `From a clinical perspective...`, `At the same time...`). |
| **2. "Victory-Lap Recap" With Zero Genuine Scientific Thinking** (re-listing H1→H7 with assertive `demonstrate/confirm/establish` + `9+` acronyms/100w) | Human CNS Discussions average **`6.7–8.8 / 1kw`** epistemic/speculative nuance (*Nature* `8.8`, *Cancer Cell* `8.6`) vs `< 5/1kw` in AI recaps! | **Use the 5 Human Scholarly Thinking Moves**: devote `1–2` paragraphs to (A) **upstream `"why/how"` hypotheses** (*what local cue induces the niche enzyme? how does receptor signalling repress target transcription?*), (B) **resolving field contradictions**, (C) **lineage/regimen boundary conditions**, (D) **concrete next-step experiments/trials**, and (E) **conversational woven caveats**. |
| **3. Formulaic `"Thus, X..."` / `"Therefore, Y..."` Slogan at Every Paragraph End** | Reads like a slide-deck summary footer (`26%` of paragraphs vs `2.9%` in *Nat Commun*!) | End most Discussion (and Results) paragraphs directly on a **concrete biological implication, literature comparison, or open mechanistic question**; reserve `"Taken together"` for the single closing horizon paragraph! |

### 2.3 Side-by-Side Anti-Stiffness Discussion Transformation (Stiff PPT Outline → Fluid *Nature* Essay)
- **Before (Stiff LLM Outline — `First/Second/Third` + Assertive Recap)**:
  `"Consistent with spatial studies, our findings extend three areas. First, whereas bulk taxonomies framed subtypes as discrete, our high-resolution data reveal opposing State A and State B programmes along a 50-µm gradient. Second, whereas unadjusted data blamed endothelial ligand, ambient correction reveals stromal cleavage. Third, our model benchmark defines an optical boundary. Thus, multimodal profiling is superior."`
- **After (Fluid Human CNS Essay — Thematic Depth + Genuine Mechanistic Thinking)**:
  `"Our findings reshape how intratumoural lineage plasticity is interpreted in solid tumours. Whereas canonical bulk taxonomies classified differentiated and mesenchymal programmes as mutually exclusive tumour-wide subtypes, high-resolution spatial profiling reveals that both states coexist within individual lesions along a steep microanatomical gradient. Why adjacent fibroblasts and macrophages selectively assemble a protease-rich paracrine niche along the tissue interface remains an open mechanistic question; one plausible explanation is that endothelial-derived cues or local metabolic gradients license enzyme induction locally. Similarly, how receptor activation directly represses target transcription warrants further dissection, although prior work raises the possibility that EMT transcription factors downstream of kinase signalling mediate this lineage switch."`
  *(Zero `First/Second/Third` bullets; zero internal code spam; natural human scientific curiosity flowing from observation → field shift → upstream mechanistic hypotheses!)*

### 2.4 Four-Corner Structural Tightness (`Title ↔ Abstract ↔ Intro ↔ Discussion`) & Results Progression Balance (`n = 162` Lossless HTML-CNS Benchmark)

Empirically distilled from **`162` lossless HTML-parsed CNS full-text papers** (*Cell / Cancer Cell / Immunity / Nat Cancer / Science / Sci Transl Med*):

1. **`Title ↔ Abstract` Signature-Term Lock (HTML-CNS med `75%`, IQR `64–87%`)**:
   - **Never disguise the Title's signature conceptual terms behind vague synonyms inside the Abstract!** When trimming acronyms in the Abstract, preserve the Title's exact anchor nouns/mechanisms verbatim (`≥ 60–75%` signature-term recall) while keeping verbatim 4-gram copying between `Abstract ↔ Intro final para ↔ Discussion P1` ultra-low (`1–2%`).
   - **Anchor Every Major Results Pillar**: If a major Figure introduces a treatment inversion or computational-pathology boundary, seed it cleanly in Intro P1–P4 and reflect it explicitly in the Abstract closing sentence rather than compressing an entire Figure into 3 vague words.
2. **Anti-Gerund-Opener Overcorrection in Results (`≤ 15%`; HTML-CNS med `0.0%`, IQR `0.0–4.6%`)**:
   - Do **NOT** eliminate `We next` by replacing 25–30% of Results paragraph openings with impersonal gerund subjects (`Scoring...`, `Integrating...`, `Combining...`, `Comparing...`, `Visualising...`).
   - Real published CNS papers keep active **`We`** inside **`60.8%` (IQR `50–71%`)** of Results paragraph opening sentences (`To [verb], we...` `12–25%`, direct `We [verb]...` `10–20%`, natural `We next/then` `5–15%`) so the investigator's scientific questions visibly drive the narrative.
3. **Mandatory 1-Clause Cross-Section Motivation Bridges (`H2 → Hk`)**:
   - Never open a new Results subsection by cold-dropping a pipeline name or cohort count (`Differential expression analysis using edgeR...`, `Across five independent cohorts...`). Always connect the previous section's open gap to the next section's assay in 1 crisp clause (Module `M14`) — especially in multi-modal studies spanning genomics, spatial imaging, murine perturbations, and digital pathology!
